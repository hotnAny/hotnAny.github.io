Why augmenting human intelligence?

There are now an army of Waymo cars on the streets of LA (and more recently, the highways as well)

What's changed is not just the number of self-driving cars, it's how people now seem to have perceived it as a norm.

Statistics don't lie, they show that robotaxis like Waymo drive better, safer than human drivers. 

As these self-driver cars populate the streets and highways, we will see fewer accidents.

No one seems to argue any more that driving can't be done by AI alone and human drivers' involvement is necessary.

No need for human-AI symbiosis in the realm of driving.

I wonder (and worry) whether the same thing will happen in other domains, such as creative and scientific work.

I work on human-AI symbiotic systems and I used to motivate my research by citing how AI alone can't accomplish domain experts' work, e.g., medical diagnosis.

But recently, I started to wonder if, or how much longer, would such arguments stand.

For creative domains, there still seems a pretty large potential-deployment gap of AI.

AI might be good at "reverse-engineering" to mimic great pieces of art that already exist but still struggles to achieve the same level or greatness when creating something original.

For scientific work, some recent breakthroughs made by latest AI models [1, 2, 3] seems to suggest that there is a trend of AI doing science like driving at some point in the future.

Admittedly, even if that future is real, we still have a long way to get there.

Foremost, in many domains like medicine and biology, breakthroughs can't be made entirely in vitro on a computer.

AI needs to be able to perform wet lab experiments at scale---think robotic experiment centers like data centers today.

But the question is, let's imagine one day AI is capable of performing scientific work, just like how it can self-drive a car today---should humans relinquish our involvement, stop studying or doing science, just let AI take over?

I feel this is becoming a philosophical or ideological question rather than a factual question.

If ends justify the means, that we have a well-defined goal (e.g., curing cancers, discovering extraterrestrial lives), then anything goes, AI-replacing-humans included.

But a different perspective is, what's the point? What's the point of doing science at all if it eventually is run by AI and humans are only by-standers. 

We do science because we want to go beyond the limits of how we are biologically programmed, to push the boundaries of our capabilities, and to understand the universe at the most fundamental level. The value of doing science is more than the objective outcome, it's how the process itself makes humans an increasingly self-improving species.
