It is hard to put my finger on what this book is about. Deutsch is a physicist known for his contributions to quantum physics, yet the book is about much more than physics. In fact, physics takes up only a small part of it; the rest ranges across mathematics, computer science, biology, and philosophy. I should also admit that I read only a subset of the chapters: Chapters 1, 4, 7, and 8.

Richard Feynman once said something to the effect that science is about trying not to fool ourselves. This book is a real testament to that idea. Deutsch has an attitude of "let's really get to the bottom of what X is about." For example, he questions the prevalent understanding of natural selection as an "optimization" process that chooses the fittest individuals and passes on their genes. Deutsch, who is not an evolutionary biologist, uses common sense and plain language to point out that evolution is really about variants of genes competing to spread themselves as widely as possible among the population of a given species. Nothing in this process guarantees optimality.

That said, reading Deutsch is exhausting. As a reader, you feel like you are constantly trying to match your own intelligence to his, which is much higher. You can feel the author's smartness, and also how often his writing falls short of conveying his ideas---or, shall we say, how the expressive capacity of human language is insufficient to carry them. Between the lines I can even read a certain impatience: he might have tried to make an idea understood, but did not want to keep trying---perhaps because the book has too many ideas that need explaining.

I stopped after those four chapters, partly because I had planned to read something else that is more relevant to my research, and partly because I simply needed a break. But I will return to read more later. As much of a pain as it is to read this book, I feel it has made me more critical, clearer in my thinking, and more willing to question assumptions. It feels like mental training for researchers, even though its material lies outside my own research field.
