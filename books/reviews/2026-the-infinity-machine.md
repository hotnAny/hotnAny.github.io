This book solidifies Demis Hassabis as my hero---being both an academic who can found a company and a tech CEO who deeply cares about research and humanity. There is no shortage of great founders in AI today, but such a unique combination makes Demis rare and valuable. As the book puts it, he was "not chiefly motivated by money or power": while Sam Altman builds OpenAI for power, Demis cares about solving humanity's biggest challenges.

Once Demis deems a problem important, he breathes solving that problem 24/7. Demis goes all in. 100% or 0%. There is no in between. In his own words: "I'm always 0 or 100 percent, right?" Shane Legg puts it even more sharply---there is no 50 percent mode in him, not even a 99 percent mode; only 100 percent.

But this duality of roles is not toll-free: Demis did admit to being distracted during fund-raising time (which is what mainly motivated him to bring DeepMind into Google) and by the chaos of corporate reorganization. I admire Demis' persistence in a romantic vision of "understanding the universe at the most fundamental level" while being often neck-deep in the muddy water of reality.

One note on its comparison with [*Empire of AI*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_AI). I hesitated before I decided to read (listen to) this book, because I had already listened to *Empire of AI* and thought it might be some repetition. But the two books couldn't be more different. Foremost, this book's author, Sebastian Mallaby, strikes me as someone who actually understands AI---his takes on the technical terms, e.g., reinforcement learning, seem to be both accurate yet descriptively simple enough for a general audience to understand (I believe Feynman also considered such an ability as equal to a deep enough understanding of the subject matter). On the other hand, *Empire of AI* never cares to get engaged with the technology; it is more concerned with the human and societal factors engendered in the development of AI.
