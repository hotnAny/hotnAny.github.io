- I selectively read a subset of the chapters. Still, it's hard for me to put my finger on what this book is about. Deutsch is a physicist known for his contribution to quantum physics. Yet the book is more than about physics. In fact, physics-related topics are only quite a small part of the book, which also touches on mathematics, computer science, biology, and philosophy.
- Richard Feynman once said something about that science is about trying not to fool ourselves. I feel this book is a real testament to this quote. Deutsch has this attitude of, "let's really get to the bottom and understand what X is about". For example, he questions the prevalent understanding that natural selection is like an "optimization" process, choosing the fittest individuals and pass on their genes. Deutsch, not a evolutionary biologist, is able to use common-sense and plain language and point out that evolution is really about variations of genes compete to spread themselves as much as possible amongst the population of a given species. There is nothing that guarantees optimality in this process.
- You can feel the smartness of the author, how his writing often falls short in conveying his idea. Or, shall we say that the expressive capacity of human language is insufficient to convey his idea. Between the lines I can even read the author's impatience: he might have tried to make his idea understood but he didn't want to keep trying---perhaps the book has too many ideas that need explanation.
