This book solidifies Demis Hassabis as my hero---being both an academic that can found company and a tech CEO that deeply cares about research and humanity.
There is no shortage of great founders in AI today but such a unique combination makes Demis rare and valuable.
As the book puts it: Sam Altman builds OpenAI for power but Demis cares about solving humanities' biggest challenges. (@AGENT: help me find the original quote if possible).
But this duality of role is not toll-free: Demis did admit being distracted during fund-raising time (which is what mainly motivated him to join DeepMind into Google) and the chaos of corporate reorganization.
I admire Demis' persistence in a romantic vision of "Understanding the universe at the most fundamental level" while being often neck-deep in the muddy water of reality.
Once Demis deems a problem as important, he breathes solving this problem 24/7 (@AGENT help me find the exact quote). Demis goers all in. 100% or 0%.. There is no in between.

One note on its comparison with the Emperor of AI. I hesitated before I decided to read (listen to) this book because I had already listened to the Emperor of AI and thought it might be some repetition. but the two books couldn't be more different. Foremost, this book's author Sebastian Mallaby strikes me as someone who actually understands AI---his takes on the technical terms, e.g., reinforcement learning, seem to be both accurate yet descriptively simple enough for general audience to understand (I believe Feynman also considered such abilities as equal to a deep enough understanding of the subject matter). On the other hand, the Emperor of AI is never cares to get engaged with the technology; it is more concerned with the human and societal factors engendered in the development of AI.
